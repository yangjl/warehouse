## 1. Executive Summary

**Xu Logistics Center** is a planned 21-acre inland intermodal facility on the east side of Interstate 29 near Bunge Avenue, in Council Bluffs, Pottawattamie County, Iowa, immediately east of Omaha, Nebraska. The facility will combine four service lines on one footprint:

1. **Container yard (primary)** — carrier-neutral storage for empty and laden marine containers with gate operations, chassis access, and light M&R.
2. **Transloading** — labor and equipment to move freight between marine containers, rail cars, bulk trucks, and dry vans, with a particular focus on bulk-to-container loading for regional ag exports.
3. **Standard warehousing** — racked and floor storage for regional distribution, kitting, labeling, and 3PL overflow.
4. **US Customs bonded warehousing** — deferred-duty storage under 19 CFR Part 19 (Class 3 public bonded warehouse is the leading candidate), with FTZ #64 subzone activation as a parallel path to evaluate.

The site is adjacent to Bunge North America's Council Bluffs soybean complex, within ~5 truck miles of Union Pacific's Council Bluffs Yard, and within reach of BNSF intermodal flows. Interstate 29, Interstate 80, and Interstate 480 form a dense highway node around the parcel.

The founder, **Mr. Xu**, brings 40+ years of ocean freight and logistics experience and 7 years owning and operating a trucking company. His carrier relationships seed the customer pipeline; his trucking company provides captive drayage from day one.

The project is structured as a **3-phase, ~30-month buildout**:

- **Phase 1 (Months 1–12)**: site work, paved container yard, gate system, transload pad, initial equipment, admin trailer. Capex: ~$13M–$22M.
- **Phase 2 (Months 12–24)**: ~80,000–120,000 sq ft standard warehouse with rack and dock equipment. Capex: ~$8M–$16M.
- **Phase 3 (Months 18–30)**: ~30,000–50,000 sq ft bonded warehouse (CBP-compliant build + IT + surety bond), optional FTZ subzone activation. Capex: ~$4M–$9M.

**Total Phase 0–3 capex (Base): ~$26M–$49M.** **Phase 1 funding ask (Base): $18M–$24M** in a blended stack of owner equity, senior CRE construction-to-perm debt, equipment financing, Iowa / Pottawattamie / Council Bluffs incentives, and optional outside equity for Phase 2 / Phase 3 acceleration.

**Year 4 stabilized revenue (Base): ~$22M; EBITDA: ~$6M–$8M (~28–35% margin).**

These figures are **DRAFT planning estimates**, not guarantees, and require Mr. Xu's review and a CPA / engineer sign-off before they are used externally.

## 2. The Opportunity

Three structural conditions make Council Bluffs / Omaha a strong setting for an inland intermodal play **right now**:

1. **Persistent empty-container imbalance in the Midwest.** Inbound consumer-goods traffic plus outbound ag commodities create a chronic mismatch where empties are scarce at the moments and locations exporters need them. An independent, carrier-friendly CY with real ocean-side relationships can solve a structural availability problem for shippers and an asset-utilization problem for carriers.
2. **Containerized ag-export growth, especially specialty soy and pork.** Bulk vessel and bulk rail dominate the largest commodity flows, but specialty (non-GMO, organic, identity-preserved) soybean and DDGS programs, plus value-added pork and beef exports, increasingly move in containers. These shippers need transload labor, predictable empties, and food-grade handling.
3. **Tariff and trade-policy environment favoring bonded / deferred-duty storage.** Sustained tariff volatility (Section 232, 301, IEEPA-based, AD/CVD) makes bonded warehouse and FTZ status more economically valuable than in low-tariff periods. Regional importers want bonded storage closer to upper-Midwest consumption nodes rather than only on the coasts.

For deeper sizing, see `doc/MARKET_RESEARCH.md`.

## 3. Customers

Five segments make up the demand:

1. **Steamship lines** seeking carrier-named inland depots near ag origins (segment 1).
2. **NVOCCs and forwarders** needing flexible CY and transload labor (segment 2).
3. **Ag exporters** needing predictable empties and food-grade transload (segment 3 — Bunge, ADM, Cargill, CHS and the specialty soy / pork cluster).
4. **Import distributors** needing bonded / FTZ storage closer to their upper-Midwest customers (segment 4 — appliances, e-mobility, furniture, automotive parts, machinery).
5. **Regional 3PLs and brokers** needing overflow capacity (segment 5).

Mr. Xu's relationships make segments 1, 3, and 4 the highest-likelihood early wins. Detail and the interview plan are in `doc/CUSTOMER_DISCOVERY.md`.

## 4. Site, Operations, And Phasing

### 4.1 Site

- 21 acres, east side of I-29, near Bunge Avenue, Council Bluffs, IA. APN, zoning letter, Phase I ESA, and survey are open items.
- Usable CY area after gates, drives, M&R, and stacking aisles: ~13–16 acres.
- Adjacent / nearby: Bunge soybean complex; UP Council Bluffs Yard (~5 truck miles); BNSF Gibson Yard flows; I-29 / I-80 / I-480 interchange; Eppley Airfield (OMA) ~5 miles west.

### 4.2 Operations

- **Container yard**: 1,000–1,300 TEU static slots, stacked appropriately by box type; OCR + RFID gate system; one M&R lane; chassis pool relationship with DCLI / TRAC or carrier-controlled pools as appropriate.
- **Transload pad**: 1–2 doors at open in Phase 1, expandable to 4–6 by Year 4; conveyor and dust-control package for ag loading; food-grade SOPs.
- **Standard warehouse**: ~80,000–120,000 sq ft tilt-up with ESFR sprinkler, 32-ft clear height, 50/52 ft × 50 ft column grid, dock doors and one drive-in, climate control as needed for selected commodities.
- **Bonded warehouse**: ~30,000–50,000 sq ft within or attached to Phase 2, CBP-grade segregation, monitored security and access control, certified scale, dedicated bonded inventory system and CBP-recordkeeping IT.
- **Captive drayage**: Mr. Xu's existing trucking company provides the first 10–15 daily drayage moves on day one and scales as customer volume grows.

## 5. Regulatory Path (Critical-Path Items)

These items drive timing and feasibility more than capex:

- **Bonded warehouse approval** (CBP, 19 CFR Part 19). Application includes warehouse description, security and recordkeeping plan, employee bond / background, and surety bond filing. Lead time typically 4–9 months after a complete application. Engage a CBP-licensed customs broker as an advisor before applying.
- **FTZ #64 subzone** (alternate / parallel path). Activation typically takes 6–12+ months and requires Foreign-Trade Zones Board concurrence and CBP activation. Evaluate which wrapper best fits the dominant tenant.
- **Zoning, site plan, and building permit** (City of Council Bluffs). Confirm I-2 (or local equivalent) zoning; site plan approval; building permit; certificate of occupancy.
- **Stormwater and environmental** (Iowa DNR). NPDES general permit for industrial stormwater; Phase I ESA and, if triggered, Phase II; wetlands and floodplain checks given Missouri River proximity.
- **Highway access** (IDOT and City). Confirm any haul-road improvements, signalization, and overweight permitting.
- **DOT / FMCSA** (Mr. Xu's trucking company). Confirm authority, MCS-150, BOC-3 and insurance suitability for integrated facility operations.

## 6. Differentiation And Competitive Risk

Differentiation rests on three pillars:

1. **Owner pedigree.** Mr. Xu's 40+ years of ocean-freight relationships shorten the carrier-approval cycle and seed the ag-export and import-distributor pipelines. A captive trucking company provides cost-advantaged drayage from day one.
2. **Multi-service stack on one footprint.** Combining CY + transload + general warehouse + bonded warehouse, with a single TOS and one billing relationship, is uncommon in this MSA. It lowers customer switching cost and increases share of wallet.
3. **Carrier-neutral positioning.** Unlike rail-operated terminals, the facility can serve both UP and BNSF flows and remain neutral as carrier alliances shift.

Risks and substitutes are catalogued in `doc/COMPETITIVE_LANDSCAPE.md`.

## 7. Business Model And Pricing

Six revenue lines, with pricing detail in `doc/BUSINESS_ASSUMPTIONS.md` (P-01 through P-09):

- CY storage (loaded / empty) and gate fees.
- Transloading throughput fees (container ↔ bulk truck, container ↔ rail, bulk ↔ container).
- Standard warehouse storage (rack and floor) plus handling.
- Bonded warehouse storage at a +25–40% premium, plus customs-related service fees.
- Captive drayage.
- Optional value-add (light M&R, labeling, kitting, fumigation through partner).

Year 4 Base revenue: ~$22M, with CY + drayage anchoring the base load and bonded + warehouse driving margin expansion in Years 3–4.

## 10. Risks And Mitigations

| Risk | Impact | Mitigation |
|------|--------|------------|
| CBP bonded warehouse application delayed | Pushes Phase 3 revenue out 6–12+ months | Pre-engage customs broker advisor; submit application during Phase 2 build; pursue FTZ subzone in parallel as backup |
| Carrier go-live slow (only 1 carrier names depot in Year 1) | Reduces Year 1–2 CY revenue 20–40% | Sequence 3–5 carrier conversations during Phase 0; LOIs before paving complete; design for multi-carrier from day one |
| Soybean / pork export volumes decline due to trade dispute | Reduces transload revenue 15–30% | Build domestic transload and import distribution offsets; bonded warehouse demand often rises in same scenario |
| Paving cost overrun (concrete vs. asphalt; geotech surprises) | $1M–$4M capex variance | Geotech early; phase paving so heavy-load areas are concrete and parking / dwell areas are asphalt; price both options |
| Driver / labor shortage and wage inflation | Adds $0.4M–$0.8M opex / year | Iowa Workforce Development partnership; CDL training pipeline; longer-term operator pool through trucking company |
| Interest rate rise during construction | DSCR pressure | Lock construction-to-perm with rate cap; size with 1.30x+ DSCR cushion; equipment lease vs. own analysis |
| Rail policy change on third-party depots | Operating-model risk | Maintain carrier-neutral positioning; diversify across UP and BNSF; build truck-only revenue mix |
| Adjacent Bunge or industrial neighbor incident | Operating disruption | Mutual-aid plan; insurance for business interruption; clear separation from food-grade product flows |

A fuller risk register lives in the internal deck (`presentations/internal/`) and is mirrored in `doc/PITCH_DECK_PLAN.md`.
