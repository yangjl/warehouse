## Slide 1 — Cover

- Business name: **Xu Logistics Center**
- Tagline: **The carrier-neutral inland gateway for Omaha / Council Bluffs**
- Audience: equity partners and senior lenders for Phase 1
- Date: 2026

Speaker notes: 21 acres on the east side of I-29 near Bunge Avenue, in Council Bluffs, Iowa. Container yard, transloading, standard and bonded warehousing. Founder: Mr. Xu, 40+ years ocean freight, 7 years trucking. Today's decision: Phase 1 capital commitment of $18M–$24M.

## Slide 2 — Problem

- Midwest exporters cannot find empties when they need them. Inland imbalance is structural.
- Coastal ports and inland rail ramps drive dwell, demurrage, and equipment penalties.
- Omaha / Council Bluffs has no carrier-neutral facility combining **container yard + transload + bonded warehouse** on one footprint.

Speaker notes: This is the single point that has come up every time Mr. Xu has talked to a Midwest exporter or a steamship line over the last decade. The "where is my empty?" call is a chronic customer-service problem for carriers and a chronic cost for shippers.

## Slide 3 — Solution

- 21-acre site, fronting I-29, next to Bunge's CB soybean complex, 5 truck miles from UP Council Bluffs Yard, within reach of BNSF flows.
- Four service lines on one footprint: **container yard** (primary), **transload pad**, **standard warehouse**, **US Customs bonded warehouse**.
- One operator. One billing relationship. Captive drayage from Mr. Xu's trucking company.

Speaker notes: The differentiator is not any single service. It's stacking them on one footprint, with one TOS, one operator, and a founder whose carrier relationships shorten the depot-approval cycle from years to months.

## Slide 4 — Why Now

- Containerized ag exports (specialty soy, DDGS, pork) are growing in the Midwest.
- Tariff and trade-policy volatility makes bonded / deferred-duty storage and FTZ status economically valuable again.
- Inland inventory regionalization is pulling 3PL demand out of LA / IE / Chicago into nodes like Omaha.
- Carrier strategies favor named off-dock depots near origin demand.

Speaker notes: We are not betting on a single tailwind. We are betting that two or three of these continue to play out over a 7-year hold.

## Slide 5 — Market

- Catchment: ~75 mi drayage radius; 250–350 mi for transload and bonded distribution.
- Sized opportunity (Base, DRAFT):
  - CY: 60,000–75,000 TEU per year stabilized.
  - Transload: 3,500–4,500 boxes per year.
  - Warehouse: ~100,000 sq ft standard + ~40,000 sq ft bonded.
- Anchors: Bunge, ADM, Cargill, CHS, Tyson, Smithfield, JBS, plus the appliance / e-mobility / automotive parts import flow.

Speaker notes: Sizing logic is in `doc/MARKET_RESEARCH.md` (slot × dwell × utilization). Confidence is medium until carrier and exporter interviews are logged.

## Slide 8 — Competition And Differentiation

- Rail-owned terminals (UP, BNSF) — captive to their own network.
- Third-party CYs in CB / Omaha — limited acreage, rarely combine all four service lines.
- Coastal inland-port operators (Chicago, KC, Dallas) — geographically displaced from upper-Midwest demand.

We differentiate on **carrier neutrality, multi-service stack, and founder pedigree**.

Speaker notes: Detail in `doc/COMPETITIVE_LANDSCAPE.md`. Risks include a national 3PL entry and Class I depot-policy changes; mitigations are diversification across UP and BNSF and a strong tenant book in the bonded layer.

## Slide 9 — Business Model

- CY storage + gate fees (per TEU per day, per move).
- Transload throughput (per container, with ag premium).
- Standard warehouse storage + handling (per pallet, per sq ft).
- Bonded storage at +25–40% premium plus customs service fees.
- Captive drayage (in-radius blended).
- Optional value-add: light M&R, kitting, labeling.

Speaker notes: Pricing detail is in `doc/BUSINESS_ASSUMPTIONS.md` (P-01 through P-09). All bands are DRAFT pending a market-rate survey.
