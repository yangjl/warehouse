Global container transportation

The relevant flow is a loop, not a one-way import lane

This slide reframes the opportunity as a loop. Imports from China and Northeast Asia create inland container presence. The economic question is whether those boxes can be turned into reliable export equipment for nearby ag and feed programs instead of simply being repositioned empty.
Evidence signals

The strongest numbers point to ag/feed transload first

U.S. containerized grain, 2024

15.2 mmt

USDA AMS reported 2024 U.S. containerized grain shipments up 13% from 2023.

DDGS and soybeans

5.1 + 5.1

DDGS and soybeans were the largest containerized grain commodities in 2024.

Iowa + Nebraska ag exports

$21.9B

USTR state profiles: Iowa $13.7B and Nebraska $8.2B in 2024 agricultural exports.

Current evidence supports a specific niche: container yard and food/feed-grade transload for ag-export programs, with import bonded storage treated as a secondary hypothesis until customer interviews prove it.

The slide intentionally avoids revenue forecasts. The numbers only show that the commodity base and containerized ag export category are real. We still need to translate national data into a Council Bluffs catchment estimate.
Niche market map

Where the niche market is

Best current hypothesis

A carrier-named, off-dock container yard and food/feed-grade transload node near Bunge Ave / I-29.

Secondary option

Bonded storage for China/Asia-origin importers is plausible, but it is not yet as well supported by customer evidence.

Need 1

Carriers

Approved depot, reliable gate, EDI, condition reporting, chassis plan.

Need 2

Ag/feed exporters

Predictable empty boxes, food-grade handling, sealing, surge capacity.

Need 3

Importers

Regional staging, duty timing, retrieval SLA, clean WMS records.

The internal conclusion is deliberately narrower than a pitch deck: do not lead with "warehouse." Lead with the point where container equipment and ag export demand meet. Bonded storage can become central only if importer interviews support it.

What is not proven

Stormwater, geotech, floodplain, Phase I ESA.

This slide is intentionally conservative. A strong internal plan should say what has not been proven. That keeps the work useful to Mr. Xu and avoids accidentally converting hypotheses into claims.
Validation plan

Next research should follow the niche, not the full wish list

Wave 1

3 carriers

Depot naming requirements, system requirements, equipment allocation, minimum volume.

Wave 2

4 ag/feed exporters

Boxes per week, harvest surge needs, food-grade SOPs, empty-box pain.

Wave 3

3 importers + broker

Bonded storage demand, duty timing, WMS controls, CBP class selection.

Decision threshold

Move forward only if at least two carriers and two ag/feed exporters show operationally specific demand, not just polite interest.

The suggested order is based on current evidence strength. Bonded warehouse work should still continue with a customs broker, but the earliest commercial validation should focus on carriers and ag/feed exporters.
