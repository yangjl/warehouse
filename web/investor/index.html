Problem

The Midwest is structurally short of containers — at exactly the wrong moments.

  • Empty-box scarcity at origin. Inbound consumer goods plus outbound ag exports create chronic imbalance. Exporters wait for empties that are not where they need to be.
  • Dwell & demurrage at congested ramps. Coastal ports and inland rail terminals impose penalties that compound across the supply chain.
  • No integrated alternative locally. Omaha / Council Bluffs has no carrier-neutral facility combining container yard, transload, and bonded warehousing on one footprint.
This is the single point Mr. Xu has heard for a decade from Midwest exporters and steamship lines. "Where is my empty?" is a chronic customer-service problem for carriers and a chronic cost line for shippers. Tie the pain to specific dollars: a delayed empty can cost an exporter a weekly vessel slot; a misplaced chassis can park a load for days. Quote a customer when interviews land in `doc/CUSTOMER_DISCOVERY.md`.
Solution

One 21-acre site. Four service lines. One operator.

Container Yard

1,000–1,300 TEU static slots. OCR + RFID gate. Chassis access. Light M&R. Carrier-neutral.

Transload

Bulk ↔ container ↔ truck ↔ rail. Food-grade SOPs. Designed for ag-export surge.

Standard Warehouse

~100,000 sq ft tilt-up. Rack & floor storage. Regional distribution, kitting, labeling.

Bonded Warehouse

~40,000 sq ft, 19 CFR Part 19 Class 3. Deferred duty. FTZ #64 subzone as alternate path.

Plus captive drayage from Mr. Xu's existing trucking company.

The differentiator is not any single service line — it is stacking them on one site, with one TOS, one billing relationship, and one operator. That lowers customer switching cost and increases share of wallet. Site adjacency to Bunge's CB soybean complex, UP Council Bluffs Yard (~5 truck miles), BNSF flows, and the I-29 / I-80 / I-480 interchange is the locational moat.
Why Now

Three tailwinds overlap, and the site is already controlled.

  • Containerized ag exports are growing. Specialty soy, DDGS, and pork programs increasingly move in boxes rather than bulk.
  • Tariff volatility makes bonded & FTZ status valuable again. Section 301, IEEPA-based, and AD/CVD duties drive demand for deferred-duty storage.
  • Inland inventory regionalization. Importers are pushing 3PL footprint out of LA / IE / Chicago into upper-Midwest nodes.
  • Carrier strategies favor named off-dock depots. Empty repositioning costs make inland depot networks more important, not less.
We do not need every tailwind to play out — two of four is enough for the Base case. The Conservative scenario in `doc/FINANCIAL_MODEL_NOTES.md` assumes a softer ag-export environment and still produces stabilized EBITDA above debt service.
Market

Sized to a 75-mile drayage radius and a 350-mile transload reach.

  • Container yard: 60,000–75,000 TEU per year stabilized (slot × dwell × utilization math in `doc/MARKET_RESEARCH.md`).
  • Transload: 3,500–4,500 boxes per year at two doors, one shift, with ag-peak surge.
  • Warehousing: ~100,000 sq ft standard + ~40,000 sq ft bonded.
  • Anchors within reach: Bunge, ADM, Cargill, CHS, Tyson, Smithfield, JBS — plus the appliance / e-mobility / automotive parts import flow into the upper Midwest.
Sizing is a slot-times-dwell exercise, not a top-down "% of TAM" guess. Walk the room through the math on the page in `doc/MARKET_RESEARCH.md`. Confidence is medium until carrier interviews convert the throughput assumptions into named accounts.
Customers & Traction

Five segments. Three early. One discovery plan.

  • Steamship lines — carrier-named inland depot for empties & laden boxes.
  • Ag exporters — predictable empties, food-grade transload, harvest surge capacity.
  • Import distributors — bonded / FTZ storage closer to upper-Midwest demand.
  • NVOCCs & forwarders — flexible CY and transload labor.
  • Regional 3PLs & brokers — overflow capacity.

Mr. Xu's relationships make carriers, ag exporters, and import distributors the first three to convert. 20-interview discovery plan in flight over 90 days.

Founder

Mr. Xu — 40+ years in ocean freight, 7 years owning his trucking company.

  • Ocean-side credibility shortens the carrier-approval cycle from years to months.
  • Direct relationships with steamship lines, ag exporters, and import distributors.
  • Captive drayage means cost-advantaged moves from day one — operational risk goes down, not up, at opening.
The founder advantage is not any one of these credentials — it is all three together. Ocean-side credibility opens the carrier conversation. The trucking company de-risks operations and economics. The 40 years cuts the sales cycle. Investors should weigh this slide heavily; this is the line on the page that is hardest to replicate.
Competition

Carrier-neutral, multi-service, founder-anchored.

  • Rail-owned terminals (UP, BNSF) — captive to their own network; not flexible third-party CYs.
  • Existing third-party CYs in CB / Omaha — smaller acreage, rarely combine all four service lines.
  • Coastal inland-port operators (Chicago, KC, Dallas) — geographically displaced from upper-Midwest demand.

Risks: a national 3PL entry; rail policy change on third-party depots. Mitigations: multi-carrier diversification and a deep bonded tenant book.

Detail in `doc/COMPETITIVE_LANDSCAPE.md`. Be candid about risks. A factual answer to "what stops Hub Group from doing this?" is the right answer to give in the room: time, founder access to carriers, and the specific 21 acres next to Bunge and the UP yard.
Business Model

Six revenue lines. Multiple cycles inside one P&L.

  • CY storage (loaded / empty) + gate fees.
  • Transload throughput (per container, ag premium).
  • Standard warehouse storage + handling.
  • Bonded storage at +25–40% premium + customs service fees.
  • Captive drayage (in-radius blended).
  • Optional value-add: light M&R, kitting, labeling, partner fumigation.

Pricing bands in doc/BUSINESS_ASSUMPTIONS.md (P-01 through P-09). All DRAFT pending a market-rate survey.

The reason this P&L is more resilient than a single-service CY or warehouse is the cycle mix: CY tracks carrier cycles, transload tracks the ag-export cycle, bonded tracks the import / tariff cycle. They are not perfectly correlated, which smooths revenue over a 7-year hold.

Critical path is regulatory, not construction: CBP bonded warehouse application typically takes 4–9 months from a complete file. We submit during Phase 2 so it lands when the building is ready. FTZ #64 subzone is the parallel option if the tenant pipeline favors that wrapper.
